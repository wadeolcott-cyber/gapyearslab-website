For people considering early retirement, already living it, or finding a different balance between work and life.

GapYears Lab helps make the financial decisions that come with greater freedom easier to understand. Our first tool, the Income Planner, brings income, federal taxes, and healthcare costs together so you can explore your options with greater confidence.

  • Households buying health insurance through the ACA Marketplace.

Why can federal income tax be zero while healthcare costs increase?

Qualified dividends and eligible long-term capital gains may receive preferential federal tax rates. In this investment-income mix, the standard deduction and 0% rate band leave no modeled federal regular income tax. But realized gains still count toward ACA modified adjusted gross income (MAGI), which can change the premium tax credit.

Nonqualified and qualified dividends are separate categories here and are not double-counted. These are realized taxable gains, not gross sale proceeds. Realizing gains is not inherently a poor decision; the example shows why it helps to consider taxes and healthcare costs together.

Assumptions behind this example
  • 2026 tax and coverage year; married filing jointly; ACA household size four; contiguous United States/DC; neither spouse claimed as a dependent.
  • Annual nonqualified dividends of $27,000 and qualified dividends of $13,000 in both cases. Realized long-term gains of $88,600 in A and $88,601 in B. No wages, retirement withdrawals, Roth conversions, other income, adjustments, capital losses, or prior loss carryforwards.
  • Base standard deduction of $32,200, with no additional age/blindness deductions or itemization. Qualified dividends and gains meet the preferential-rate requirements.
  • Hypothetical monthly selected plan premium of $2,500 and benchmark Silver premium of $3,000, January–December. These are not real Marketplace quotes.
  • All required premium tax credit eligibility conditions are assumed satisfied, with full-month qualifying Marketplace coverage, lawful presence, and no other qualifying coverage eligibility, special allocations, or unsupported arrangements. Advance credits are explicitly $0 in every month.
  • The 2025 poverty guideline for this household is $32,150, used for 2026 coverage. Its modeled 400% threshold is $128,600. Household size and location change the threshold.
  • Federal tax means regular pre-credit income tax only. State/local, payroll/self-employment, NIIT, AMT, other credits, and unsupported tax treatments are excluded. Net insurance cost is annual selected-plan premiums less final modeled credit, not medical out-of-pocket spending or premiums paid during the year.

This hypothetical example uses 2026 ACA rules, a married-filing-jointly household of four, and specified Marketplace premiums. Whole-dollar results reflect the reviewed draft annual Form 8962 method. The Income Planner uses continuous calculations and may display slightly different amounts. Actual eligibility and tax-return results depend on household circumstances and final IRS instructions.

This is a threshold-specific example, not a universal outcome or guaranteed tax-return result. Not every additional dollar of income causes a large subsidy loss.
