BUILT FOR THE YEARS BETWEEN

Who is GapYears Lab for?

For people considering early retirement, already living it, or finding a different balance between work and life.

The current Income Planner is primarily relevant to households whose income decisions interact with ACA Marketplace coverage before Medicare.

  • Households buying health insurance through the ACA Marketplace.
  • People evaluating Roth conversions or taxable investment sales.
  • Semi-retired people managing part-time employment or consulting income.
  • Anyone exploring how federal income taxes and healthcare costs connect.

The planner does not determine comprehensive insurance eligibility or replace professional financial or tax advice. Payroll and self-employment taxes are not modeled; consulting-income situations may require additional review.

The modeled ACA subsidy cliff

In this specific example, one cent of additional annual modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) crosses the modeled 400% federal poverty guideline threshold and eliminates the estimated premium tax credit.

Assumptions behind this example

Tax and coverage year 2026; married filing jointly; ACA household of four; contiguous United States; qualifying Marketplace coverage January–December. Each month uses a $1,000 selected plan premium and a $1,200 benchmark Silver premium.

Income consists entirely of taxable retirement withdrawals ($128,600.00 or $128,600.01), with no other income, adjustments, or itemized deductions. The reference uses the $32,200 standard deduction and the $32,150 household poverty guideline for 2025. It assumes lawful presence, qualifying full-month coverage, and no other minimum essential coverage eligibility. No advance-credit reconciliation is shown.

A threshold-specific example, not a general rule. The exact threshold depends on household size and location. Not every additional dollar of income causes a subsidy cliff. These are stored planning-model reference estimates, not a screenshot, personal recommendation, or observed live-session readings. Actual federal tax returns and Marketplace determinations may differ.

State/local income taxes and payroll/self-employment taxes are not modeled.

THE YEARS BETWEEN ARE DIFFERENT

Retirement isn't
one single chapter.

The stretch between leaving work and drawing traditional retirement benefits comes with its own questions.

Your income changes shape.

Savings, part-time work, and future benefits may each play a role at different times.

Conversions have tradeoffs.

A taxable Roth conversion can change annual income, with consequences for income taxes and ACA premium tax credits.

The pieces affect each other.

Withdrawals can affect taxes and healthcare costs. A useful plan looks at the connections.
