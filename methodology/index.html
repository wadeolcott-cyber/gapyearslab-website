OUR APPROACH

Clear assumptions. Checkable estimates.

Understand what we test, what the results mean, and where the Income Planner’s limits still matter.

Supported planning year: 2026. The Income Planner currently models 2026 federal income tax and ACA rules, using 2025 poverty guidelines for 2026 Marketplace coverage.

How we check our calculations

Different checks provide different kinds of evidence. Together they help us review supported planning scenarios; they do not establish complete tax-return accuracy.

  1. Independently derived 2026 reference cases. Expected planning results are derived separately from the application’s calculation code, then compared with its outputs.
  2. Automated regression tests. Tests check defined behaviors and safeguards so changes are less likely to alter previously checked results unexpectedly.
  3. External federal tax comparisons. The financial validation report records 59 regular federal income tax comparisons matching Tax-Calculator 6.8.5 for supported scenarios. That comparison does not validate ACA eligibility or a complete tax return.
  4. Manual checks through the deployed app. The merged November 5, 2026 report records a human tester’s verification of eight financial scenario groups, with all eight reported as matching expected reference results. The report preserves that tester attribution; it is not an independently reproduced, field-by-field audit.
  5. Documented limits. The application documents unsupported financial situations, assumptions that require review, and known differences between planning estimates and filing methods.

The homepage’s 2026 threshold example

The comparison transcribes stored reference cases C07 and C08, not invented results or a fabricated application screenshot. Both use married filing jointly, an ACA household of four in the contiguous United States, taxable retirement withdrawals only, and all twelve months of qualifying Marketplace coverage.

The monthly selected plan and benchmark Silver premiums are $1,000 and $1,200. With no other income, adjustments, or itemization, the cases use a $32,200 standard deduction and $32,150 household poverty guideline (2025 guidelines for 2026 coverage). Other coverage and eligibility assumptions are held fixed.

C07 is exactly at the modeled 400% poverty guideline threshold: $128,600.00 in annual MAGI. C08 is one cent above it. The reference credit changes from $1,591.44 to $0.00; combined federal income tax and net insurance cost changes from $21,480.56 to $23,072.00, displayed to cents. Federal income tax and net insurance cost are shown separately on the homepage.

This is a threshold-specific effect. Household size and location change the threshold, and an extra dollar of income does not always produce a cliff. Net insurance cost means selected plan premiums less the final modeled credit, not out-of-pocket medical spending or an advance-credit settlement.

What the validation does not establish

The application uses regular pre-credit federal income tax estimates and conditional ACA assumptions. Federal estimates use marginal rate schedules rather than final IRS Tax Table bands. ACA estimates use continuous poverty-guideline ratios and contribution calculations; final-year filing instructions, rounding, eligibility, and Form 1095-A records can produce different results.

Planning estimates may differ from actual federal tax returns and Marketplace determinations. These checks are not independent certification, IRS approval, professional tax approval, or comprehensive financial validation.

Current scope and important exclusions

The Income Planner supports annual income modeling, estimated federal income taxes, ACA premium tax credits, Roth conversion exploration, standard investment gains and losses, and scenario comparisons for 2026.

It does not provide state or local income tax calculations, payroll or self-employment tax calculations, complete tax-return preparation, or full health insurance eligibility determinations. Other exclusions include AMT, NIIT, other federal credits, and certain special investment and deduction treatments.

It does not provide comprehensive multi-year retirement projections, portfolio longevity calculations, or Social Security optimization. Unsupported cases and independently supplied eligibility assumptions need review before relying on estimates.
