# Early Retirement Calculator: focused SEO and educational copy

## Inspection and decisions

Reviewed the calculator HTML, model/UI modules, shared styles, existing tests, homepage, Planning Tools, Learn, all three published articles, sitemap and indexing conventions.

Existing strengths: descriptive “Early Retirement Calculator | GapYears Lab” title, unique meta description, matching social descriptions, correct production canonical/Open Graph URL, one H1, sitemap coverage, public indexability with preview-host protection, accessible inputs/results/disclosures, detailed model/privacy caveats, and contextual links from the homepage, Planning Tools and taxable-brokerage article. These are preserved.

Content gaps: the opening did not directly identify the free calculator or the full range of editable assumptions. The reference example was available through Load example but lacked a readable, static explanation of the separate overall-funding and taxable-accessibility checks. Learn had no direct contextual calculator link.

Use the requested introduction while retaining the directional-estimate disclosure directly below it; the question-form H1 does not establish an exact retirement-readiness answer. Keep all metadata as-is because no clear benefit justifies changing it. Add one natural Learn link; the brokerage article already has the relevant calculator CTA, so do not duplicate it. The ACA and Roth articles focus on annual tax/healthcare decisions and already link to related educational material and the Income Planner; no forced calculator keyword links are added there.

User-supplied Search Console baseline for November 12–18, 2026: 31 impressions, 0 clicks, average position 67.8. This was not independently fetched and does not establish a cause or prove these changes will improve rankings. No ranking/traffic outcome is promised.

## Before and after copy

**H1 before:** How much might you need to retire early?

**H1 after:** How much money do you need to retire early?

**Introduction before:** Start with what you expect to spend and the income you can count on. See how much your portfolio may need to cover before and after Social Security, then check whether your taxable savings can fund the years you plan to rely on them.

**Introduction after:** Use this free early retirement calculator to estimate your retirement savings target and determine whether your taxable brokerage and cash can help cover the years before you begin withdrawing from retirement accounts.

**New supporting paragraph:** Explore how annual spending, dependable income, Social Security timing, withdrawal rates, and investment returns affect your estimates.

**Unchanged disclosure:** Directional estimates in today’s dollars. No account, saved inputs, or financial-data upload.

**New Learn sentence:** Ready to put these ideas into numbers? Use the Early Retirement Calculator to explore your savings target and taxable bridge.

The new post-calculator section is titled “An early retirement example: total savings and your taxable bridge”. It uses the existing hypothetical Load example assumptions, explains its two results with a semantic table, and preserves the distinction between illustrative estimates, personal recommendations and success guarantees.

## Example derivation and verification

Publication amounts were generated by calling the unchanged `calculate(EXAMPLE)` function. Its inputs remain spending $75,000, dependable outside income $25,000, Social Security $30,000 beginning in 10 years, taxable savings $250,000, retirement savings $850,000, bridge 7 years, withdrawal rate 4%, real return 0%.

The engine returns combined savings $1,100,000, overall target $1,000,000 and surplus $100,000; taxable bridge target $350,000 and shortfall $100,000. The independent existing reference test confirms these outputs. Arithmetic cross-check: spending gap $50,000 for 10 pre-benefit years plus $20,000/4% ongoing target = $1,000,000 at zero real return; seven bridge years at $50,000 = $350,000. This is an explanation of the existing method, not a second model or formula change. The bridge is never added to the overall target.

The static example cannot alter user results or Load example behavior. It explicitly labels hypothetical assumptions, constant returns/spending, omitted sale-tax/ACA effects, and no guarantee. An added regression test compares its published figures with the existing engine and guards against drift in the example assumptions. All existing independent model reference tests remain.
