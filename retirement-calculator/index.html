Why taxable savings matter for early retirement

How this estimate works

Today’s purchasing power

Spending, dependable outside income, and household Social Security are ongoing amounts in today’s dollars. We assume they stay constant after inflation over their applicable periods. This does not mean every pension has inflation increases. Temporary income, pensions that end, and complex benefit timing are not modeled.

Beginning-of-year withdrawals

The annual portfolio gap is spending minus dependable outside income, with a floor of zero. Once Social Security starts, its benefit reduces that gap, also to a minimum of zero. When benefits are zero, their timing is ignored.

We discount the first years’ spending gaps using your constant annual return after inflation. At the start of the Social Security year, the ongoing gap divided by the starting withdrawal rate estimates a portfolio target. That future target is discounted back and combined with the earlier gaps. Without Social Security, the current gap divided by the rate is the target.

The illustrative 4% withdrawal-rate default is editable and not a guaranteed safe rate. Your assumed return includes dividends and interest, so those portfolio distributions must not also be counted as dependable outside income.

Taxable bridge coverage

We separately discount the spending gaps during the years you plan to rely on taxable savings, reducing the gap if Social Security begins during that period. The bridge target is not added to the overall target. A zero-year bridge needs no bridge funding. Social Security timing and bridge timing remain independent.

Returns are constant and shared across taxable and retirement assets. A 0% real-return baseline is always shown; a nonzero entered return adds a separate scenario. Bars share a percentage scale within each result section, with each scenario’s own target defining 100%.

What this does not model

  • Retirement success probabilities, longevity, or a specific retirement end date.
  • Market volatility, sequence-of-returns risk, or changing spending.
  • Future tax laws or healthcare-cost changes.
  • Social Security claiming optimization, eligibility, or benefit calculations.
  • Pensions or other dependable income that change or end.
  • Retirement-account taxes, access penalties, restrictions, or required minimum distributions.

This is a deterministic estimate, not a Monte Carlo simulation, lifetime cash-flow plan, or individualized recommendation. Savings above a target do not establish that retirement is financially secure.
